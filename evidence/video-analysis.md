## What the clip does not contain

This list matters more than the timeline, because the post uses this clip to
carry a 69% claim.

- **No tactile signal.** No sensor overlay, no force plot, no contact
  highlighting — nothing that would let a viewer see the fingertip feedback the
  method is named for. The fingertips appear rigid and metallic; whether they
  are instrumented cannot be determined from video.
- **No baseline.** No side-by-side against a policy without tactile input. A
  successful run tells you nothing about what the tactile channel contributed.
- **No failure or recovery.** The claimed mechanism is *adjusting during
  contact* — precisely what a disturbance or a slip would reveal. Nothing is
  perturbed.
- **No trial count.** One take. 69% implies a denominator that is nowhere shown.
- **Unknown playback speed.** The post states that official project videos run
  at 5x. If this is one of them, the real motion took roughly 100 s, and the
  apparent smoothness of the transport is an artefact of playback. If it is not,
  the speed is simply unstated. Either way it is unknown, and reading dexterity
  off this clip is unsafe.
- **No liquid physics evidence.** The dispense window (12–14 s) shows the tip
  inside the beaker but the beaker's contents at 19 s are not clearly changed at
  this resolution. Whether liquid actually transferred is not visually
  established.

## Inference, flagged as such

The hand is far more articulated than an Inspire hand — many visible finger
joints, independent-looking phalanges. It is **not** the Inspire hand our own
G1 model carries. Any comparison between this demo and our hardware therefore
crosses an embodiment boundary before it crosses a method boundary.

## Verdict

The clip is a competent single-take demonstration of a pipette pick-transport-
release. It is consistent with the claim and evidence for none of it. Treating
it as support for 69% average success across three tasks is a category error —
the same one [reproduction-001](../../reproduction-001) documented when 20
episodes read 80% and 500 episodes read 62%.
